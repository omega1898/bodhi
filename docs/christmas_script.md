# A Light in the Window: the script

> **What this is:** every word the player reads in the Christmas game, chapter by chapter. The plot, the world and the cast are in the story bible, `docs/christmas_story.md`; this file is the words. Chapter One is written in full, because it is built first and it is free. Chapters Two to Twelve have their key scenes written; each is filled in completely when it is built.
>
> **Written 25 November 2026** as a deeper pass on the approved story. Nothing in the plot changes. A few threads are added underneath it (a prologue, the history of the valley, Tam's dog Moss, the church bell called Mercy, the second half of the old saying, and two small guilts). Each is marked **(added)** in the bible so the owner can say no to any of them.

## 1. How the story is told

The owner asked for the story to be written the way George R. R. Martin writes: big, dark, grounded and gripping. His books are not for children and this game is, so it takes his tools, not his cruelty. Nobody dies (`docs/christmas_story.md`, section 1). These are the tools, in plain words, so whoever writes the next chapter can use them too.

1. **Open with someone else's eyes.** His books start with a prologue seen by a stranger who meets the danger before the heroes do. Ours: old Tam on the moor, the night before the first snow, watching his reindeer turn their tails to the north. The player knows the storm is coming before Em does, so Chapter One's perfect day feels fragile from the first minute.
2. **The world is older than the story.** Hollin has history the player only glimpses: the drovers who walked the path behind the cabin for three hundred years, the Long White of 1974, a dog called Moss, a bell called Mercy. Glimpses make a place feel real. Explaining everything makes it a brochure.
3. **Old words come true.** "A light for the hill" sounds like a quaint custom in Chapter One. By Chapter Eleven it is the only thing between Em and the dark. The young think old customs are decoration; the old remember why they started.
4. **Small kindnesses, large consequences.** Em lets Bodhi's collar out one notch after his towel, so it won't pinch his fluffed-up curls. That kindness is why the collar slips off in the storm. Mina is sure she latched the log store. She didn't. Neither of them is a villain. That is what makes it hurt, and what makes forgiving each other in Chapter Seven matter.
5. **Everyone is right from where they stand.** Ama calls off the search at two in the morning. She is right: they would freeze. Em hates her for it for a day, and then says so, and they are friends again. There are no villains; there are people who see different parts of the same night.
6. **The reader knows more than the hero.** The player plays Bodhi's half before Em's, so the player always knows where he is and Em doesn't. Every near miss is felt twice.
7. **Promises are plot.** "I'll bring him home in this," Em tells Mina, holding Biscuit's empty collar. She has no right to promise it. The whole second half is her keeping it.
8. **Every chapter ends on a turn.** The last line answers the chapter's question and opens a sharper one. Never end on a sigh.
9. **Name things. Feed people.** Not "food" but sausage ends and the heel of a black pudding. Not "a bell" but Mercy, cast in 1868. Specific things make a world solid, and a dog who reads the world by smell makes every chapter a list of exact, delicious things.
10. **One voice per eye.** Em speaks in the past tense, looking back; she never names her fear, she says what she did. Bodhi speaks in the present, in fragments, smell first; he never says "I". The prologue and the epilogue are a storyteller's voice, the only time the game speaks from outside the two of them.

**Rules for every caption** (unchanged from the bible, section 7): at most 90 characters, most under 60; one idea per line; say what the screen can't show; one concrete thing per line; a short line where the feeling lands.

**Voices in the tables below:** **T** is the storyteller (prologue and epilogue only), **Em** is Em, **B** is Bodhi (shown in small capitals), **N** is something written down. `{dog}` becomes his name.

## 3. Chapter One: First Snow (14 December, free)

The chapter's question: *will this be the perfect first Christmas?* Its answer: no, and the player's own thumb is what proves it.

**What runs underneath.** Five things are planted here and paid off later. Nobody notices them on a first play; everyone remembers them on the second.
- **The bell on his collar** ("so I'd always know where he was"): Chapter One's last image, Chapter Six's tears, Chapter Eleven's first sound.
- **The notch.** After the towel, Em lets his collar out one notch. Wet curls lie flat. That is how the collar comes off in the storm.
- **Biscuit looking at the hill.** An old herd dog, born on a farm, who hears weather he can't hear. Tam explains it in Chapter Six.
- **Mercy**, the church bell, "rung once, long ago, for the hill". Mina has it rung in Chapter Eleven.
- **"I shut the log store. I'm sure I did."** Mina didn't. She confesses in Chapter Seven.

### Chapter Two: The Lamp in the Window (14 December, night, to dawn), in full

Written in full on 25 November 2026, when the chapter was built. The chapter's question: *will Bodhi find Biscuit, and will anyone find Bodhi?*

*Underneath:* the first choice Bodhi ever makes for someone else. Home is up the hill, a light he can see, and the old dog can't stand. The player is offered both, and the story lets him try to go before he stays. Em carries the empty collar in her pocket, so the bell rings every time she moves. At dawn, Mina holds out her hand for Biscuit's collar and Em doesn't give it to her: she has made a promise, and she means to keep it herself. Then the old saying turns out to have a second half. And the cruellest near miss in the game: Em is at the yard fence, bent over Biscuit's collar, when the lorry with both dogs in it drives away below her.

### Chapter Four: Strand (16 December), in full

Written in full on 25 November 2026, when the chapter was built. The chapter's question: *will they find each other in a town full of people?*

*Underneath:* Biscuit leads them to a red door on Kirk Wynd, the house where he was young, and sits down to wait for it to open. It never does. A boy comes out of the house next door with a paper cone of chestnuts, and that is how Kofi comes to know two lost dogs. At the red door Bodhi understands what Em already knows, that you can't go back, and from here he leads. Then the loneliest near miss in the game: a whole town counting down together, Em's whistle and the band's first note at the same moment, and one row of huts between them.

### Chapter Eleven: Christmas Eve (24 December), in full

Written in full on 25 November 2026, when the chapter was built. The chapter's question: *will they get home?* The climax.

*Underneath:* Bodhi does what Moss did fifty years ago, on the same path, in the same storm, toward the same lit windows. The roles reverse: the dog Em carried home in Chapter One now leads her, and the player plays him, with Em following on the harness. The old saying is now literal. The custom the young thought was decoration, a light in the window for anyone still out on the hill, is the only thing in the world. Mina, who never latched the log store, has turned her guilt into a village: she found the church's old storm book, had Mercy rung, and went door to door until every house had a candle lit.
